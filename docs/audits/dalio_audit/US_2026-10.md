## 1. Verdicts

| Chip | Dashboard says | Verdict | Falsifier |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Growth | **Growth** | `CONFIRMED-WITH-CAVEAT` | Flips to `DISPUTED-LABEL` if the Sept/Oct payroll collapse (+29k, −60k back-revisions, U3 4.2%) pulls the growth composite below `gz` once ingested, or if `cfnai_ma3` prints below −0.35 for two consecutive months. |
| Inflation | **Transition** | `CONFIRMED-WITH-CAVEAT` | Flips to `DISPUTED-LABEL` if the 14 Oct CPI and late-Oct PCE confirm core re-accelerating toward the SEP's 3.4% while our 90m composite stays negative — i.e. our relative frame reads "cooling" while realised core climbs. |

**The one thing the owner must hear:** both chip *labels* survive independent scrutiny, and the growth composite shows no lag against the best external benchmark (Spearman 0.76–0.78 at lag 0). But the **inflation score is a window artifact**: the same month reads **−0.310 at 90m, −0.904 at 60m, and +0.040 on full history**. The canonical 90-month window is dominated by the 2021–23 shock, so "below its own norm" currently means "below the worst inflation in forty years" — while the Fed has just hiked into 3.4% headline CPI for the first time since 2023. The label is right; the number underneath it will mislead a reader about level.

## 3. Tier 1 — numeric benchmarks

> Benchmarks are independently *constructed*, not input-*independent*. CFNAI shares
> four inputs with our growth basket; the CPI measures re-aggregate the same BLS
> price quotes. Correlation is partly mechanical. The informative content is below.

**Growth: AGREE 4 / PARTIAL 6 / CONTRADICT 0** · **Inflation: AGREE 2 / PARTIAL 3 / CONTRADICT 0**

**Zero contradictions on either axis.** Reported as a result, not spun as a triumph:
most PARTIALs are Neutral-vs-directional pairings, which is a weak form of assent.

**CFNAI-MA3, called out separately** (the only standardised, publisher-thresholded
benchmark, so the sharpest test of our `gz` calibration): value **+0.01**, rolling Z
**+1.18**, state **Neutral**, verdict **PARTIAL**, Spearman **0.761 at lag 0**.

This single row is the most instructive in the audit. CFNAI is constructed so that
**zero equals trend growth**, and on the publisher's own ±0.70 thresholds +0.01 is
dead-centre normal. Yet its rolling Z is +1.18, because 2024–25 ran persistently
below trend. So our "Above its own norm" read and CFNAI's "at trend" read are *both
correct* — we are measuring recovery from a sub-trend stretch, not an above-trend
boom. Agent A reached this independently and called it the weakest link in its own
read.

### Lead/lag — no growth lag, large inflation lead

| Axis | Benchmark | r(0) | best lag | r(best) |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Growth | `cfnai_diffusion` | 0.783 | **0** | 0.783 |
| Growth | `cfnai_ma3` | 0.761 | **0** | 0.761 |
| Growth | `wei` | 0.702 | +1 | 0.712 |
| Inflation | `median_cpi` | **0.172** | **+6** | 0.310 |
| Inflation | `trimmed_pce` | **0.221** | **+6** | 0.284 |
| Inflation | `trimmed_cpi` | **0.199** | **+5** | 0.267 |

Growth: coincident alignment, no systematic lag. **This is a clean pass** — the
growth composite is neither late nor front-running.

Inflation: coincident correlation is **weak (0.17–0.22)** and best fit sits 5–6
months ahead. Our inflation composite appears to lead the robust trend measures by
roughly half a year — plausibly `breakeven_avg` (Z +0.69, rising) and `crude_oil`
(Z +1.10, rising) pulling the composite ahead of realised price trend. But r = 0.31
even at the best lag is weak enough that "genuine leading indicator" and "poorly
correlated" are not yet distinguishable. Punch-list item 2.

### Disagreement episodes — the biggest one, explained

**`sticky_core_cpi`, 2004-01 → 2006-03 (27 months)**: our Z +1.47 against benchmark
Z −1.10. The largest single divergence in the panel, and it is explicable: 2004–06
was an energy and commodity-led inflation impulse (crude roughly tripled) with
sticky, slow-moving domestic prices lagging well behind. Our basket carries
`crude_oil` and `ppi_broad` directly, so it registered the impulse immediately; the
sticky-price gauge is built to exclude exactly that. **This is the same structural
situation as today** — crude +53% YoY, PPI +9.85%, sticky core at 2.96% — which
makes the episode a live precedent rather than a historical curiosity, and raises the
question of whether our inflation basket is over-weighted to the fast, supply-side
leg. Related punch-list item 2.

Second-largest, `sticky_core_cpi` 2023-11 → 2025-01 (15 months, our Z −0.67 vs +1.16):
the mirror image — we read the 2023–24 disinflation faster than sticky prices
delivered it. Consistent with a genuine 5–6 month lead, not with noise.

### Level-vs-relative divergences — findings, not errors

**Growth.** Our relative "Above" and CFNAI's publisher-threshold "at trend" are both
true. The honest statement of our own chip is *"activity has recovered to trend from
a sub-trend stretch and is improving"* — not *"the economy is running hot."*

**Inflation — the central finding.** Our −0.310 says below-norm. Measured against
the 2% target, inflation is **above** it on every gauge (trimmed PCE 2.19, trimmed
CPI 2.58, median CPI 2.58, sticky core 2.96, headline 3.4). Measured against full
history our own composite is **+0.040** — dead average. The −0.310 exists only
because the 90-month window is dominated by 2021–23:

| Window | 2026-10-03 inflation score | Would imply |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Full history | **+0.040** | at norm |
| 120m | −0.070 | at norm |
| **90m (canonical)** | **−0.310** | below norm → Disinflation but for momentum |
| 60m | **−0.904** | firmly below norm |
| 48m | −0.622 | below norm |
| 36m | −0.509 | below norm |

A **0.94-point spread** on the same month, from the choice of window alone. The 60m
window reaches back only to Oct 2021 — almost entirely inside the shock — and is the
most negative. This is the finding of the audit.

**Where the chip gets real credit:** the momentum gate. Δ is +0.0153, which blocks a
Disinflation call, and the Fed (SEP core PCE 3.0 → 3.4%), the OECD (*"begun to rise
again"*) and the Cleveland Fed nowcast (core PCE 3.02 → 3.10%) all independently
say the direction is **up**. The dual-condition design caught a turn that the
Z-score alone would have missed. Likewise the **divergence flag firing TRUE** sits
squarely alongside BofA's *"mild stagflation"* characterisation.

### Strongest case against our own verdicts

**Against the Growth verdict:** we are grading a chip on Aug-2026 vintage data one
day after a September jobs report showing +29k, a 12-month average of +45k, July
revised negative and U3 at 4.2%. Our growth basket is labour-heavy — `payrolls` at
importance 0.64 plus unemployment, job openings and participation, which Ray already
flagged in the 2026-07-05 review as over-represented versus the output side. Once
September ingests, a labour-led deterioration could pull the composite under
gz 0.226 fast. A stricter auditor would call the Growth label **stale-but-not-yet-wrong**
and grade it `DISPUTED-TIMING` pre-emptively. I did not, because judging a chip
against data it legitimately cannot have is exactly the error this skill exists to
prevent — but the risk is real and asymmetric.

**Against the Inflation verdict:** `CONFIRMED-WITH-CAVEAT` may be too generous. A
reader looking at −0.310 would reasonably conclude inflation is cooling, in the same
week the Fed hiked for the first time in three years and the OECD said inflation has
begun rising again. If the purpose of a chip is to leave the reader with an accurate
impression, the inflation chip arguably fails on level even while passing on label,
and `DISPUTED-LABEL` would be defensible.
